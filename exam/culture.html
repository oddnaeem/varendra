    • [-] 1. Definition of Culture Slide 2
      Edward Tylor's Definition: "Culture refers to that complex whole which includes knowledge, beliefs, art, morals, law, customs, and any other capabilities and habits acquired by man as a member of society."
    • [-] 2. Types of Culture Slides 3-4
      • Material Culture
        • Definition: Concrete and tangible things created and used by humans.
        • Range: Prehistoric stone tools of primitive man to modern advanced computers.
        • Examples: Books, buildings, physical objects used by future generations to understand us.
      • Non-Material Culture
        • Definition: Words used, habits followed, ideas, customs, behaviors professes/conformed to.
        • Includes: Laws, techniques, lifestyle, and knowledge.
        • Examples: Beliefs, values, religions, ethics, and philosophies.
    • [-] 3. Characteristics of Culture Slides 5-7
      • • Culture is learned and acquired
      • • Culture is shared and transmitted
      • • Culture is social
      • • Culture is symbolic
      • • Culture tends toward integration
      • • Culture is cumulative
      • • Culture varies from society to society
      • • Culture keeps on changing
    • [-] 4. Components of Culture Slides 8-12
      • 1. Symbols
        Anything carrying a particular recognized meaning. Meaning varies across societies, within a society, and over time.
      • 2. Language
        • System of symbols allowing communication (written, spoken, or non-verbal).
        • Key to Cultural Transmission (passing culture across generations; historically oral tradition).
        • Cultural Variation Examples:
          - U.S.: Number 13 = bad luck (high-rises skip 13th floor).
          - Japan: Number 4 = unlucky (pronounced like 'death').
      • 3. Ideas, Beliefs, & Values
        • Ideas: Non-material conceptions of physical, social, and cultural world.
        • Beliefs: Personal convictions about a certain idea.
        • Values: Abstract concepts of what is important/worthwhile (general standards of good/bad).
      • 4. Norms
        • Behaviors reflecting compliance with defined standards of propriety, morality, legality, ethics.
        • Dictate actions in specific situations.
        • Enforced via sanctions when violated (e.g., law).
      • 5. Material Culture
        Including physical infrastructure and technology.
    • [-] 5. Cultural Integration & Local Context Slides 13-14
      • Cultural Integration Definition
        Exchange where one group assumes beliefs/rituals of another without losing its own culture (e.g., U.S. adopting Italian pizza, Mexican tacos, Chinese food).
      • Bangladesh Case Studies
        • Linguistic Unity: Bangla language unites people regardless of geography (Sylhet to Khulna).
        • National Festivals: Pahela Baishakh as an ultimate Secular Festival (non-religious celebration where Muslims, Hindus, Buddhists, Christians celebrate together).
    • [-] 6. Cultural Variation & Culture Shock Slides 15-17
      • Cultural Variation
        Rich diversity in social patterns (music, cuisine, dance). Example: Western firm handshake vs. Japanese bow.
      • Culture Shock & Stressors
        Multifaceted stress experience in contact with a new culture (common among refugees/students) [Dodge, 1990].
        • Reasons: Previous experiences, cultural distance degree, preparation level, support networks, individual psychology.
      • The "W" Curve Model (Process of Adjustment)
        Home Country → Leaving Home → Arrival in Host Country → Culture Shock → Feeling Unhappy → Adjusting → Completion of Studies → Leaving Host Country → Arrival Back Home → Reverse Culture Shock → Re-Adjusting → Adaptation to Life in Home Country.
    • [-] 7. Cultural Evaluation Perspectives Slides 18-21
      • Ethnocentrism
        Regarding one's own culture as the center/superior. Example: City dwellers incorrectly seeing rural people as "uncultured".
      • Xenocentrism
        Believing one's culture is inferior compared to others. Example: Idealizing Paris fashion, assuming French products are inherently better.
      • Cultural Relativism & Its 4 Advantages
        Assessing a culture by its own standards/local context (e.g., eating insects as a delicacy).
        • Advantage 1: Promotes cooperation (stronger societal bonds).
        • Advantage 2: Encourages respect & equality.
        • Advantage 3: Preserves human cultures.
        • Advantage 4: Creates a non-judgmental society.
